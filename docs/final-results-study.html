Four ways to sign a result

Final results combines each reciter's judge-owned sections into one official mark. Today the screen asks a judge to press Finalize without ever showing the number that press produces — the total appears only after it has been committed. Four candidates, each borrowed from somewhere this problem is already solved. All four show the marks first; that is not the variable under test.

01

The problem

finalizeParticipantResult() reads a score and a maximum for every criterion, names the judge who produced each, and sums them. Every one of those numbers exists when the row renders. None of them reaches the screen: the slot where a total belongs holds one of four grey words — Ready, Needs judge result, Participant details missing, Source changed — until the moment after finalizing, when the total finally appears.

When one criterion has two candidate sessions, the cell becomes a <select> reading Judge 2 · revision 1. The two marks that differ — the only reason a choice exists — are not shown. The judge picks a person rather than a number.

All four candidates render the same six reciters, carrying every state the real screen has to survive: four sections in and ready; a criterion with two competing sessions; a criterion missing altogether; a result already finalized; and one whose source was reopened and corrected after it was finalized.

Reciter #07 is the conflict case in all four. Resolve it, finalize it, and the counters under each candidate move — they are measured from the DOM at run time, not typed in.

Why every criterion is named in words here, never by its colour alone. Running the four category colours through a CVD check puts Jalī #d8453d and Khafī #c0892a at ΔE 6.9 under deuteranopia — inside the 6–8 floor band, legal only alongside a second encoding. Khafī also measures 2.99:1 against the light surface, under the 3:1 a mark needs. The stylesheet already states the principle at .queue-group: state is carried by the word as well as the colour. On a results sheet that is not a nicety — the two criteria with the closest colours are the two worth the most marks.

Four candidates

AProtocol sheetFigure skating

One row per reciter, one column per criterion, the judge named under each mark. After every skate the ISU publishes the full arithmetic — every element, every judge's grade, the trimmed mean, the total — so anyone can re-add it. The same shape here lets a chief judge read down a column, which is the only way severity is ever noticed by eye.

04

Against the grammar

Every rule below is one the app already keeps somewhere. All four candidates keep the proposed rule 11 — a number is visible before it is committed — because that is the defect this study exists to close, not a variable to test.

A — Protocol sheet

Keeps

  • Rule 6 — a grid of marks with named columns needs no sentence above it. The four grey status words are gone; the state is the content of the cell.
  • Rule 9 — every criterion is named in text beside its number, so the ΔE 6.9 Jalī/Khafī pair is never load-bearing.
  • Rule 3 — no colour at all. Nothing here is a verdict the app has not already recorded.

Bends

  • Rule 10, 44px targets — the conflict chooser is a 24px pill. Bent under amendment 2: this screen is never open during a recitation, and 44px rows would halve what a chief judge can compare at once. Finalize itself stays 34px and destructive actions keep the full 44.
  • Rule 7, nothing unreachable — the sheet scrolls sideways under about 780px. Real, and the reason the reciter meta is one line rather than two.

B — Reconciliation queue

Keeps

  • Rule 1 — the decision is the work and gets the room; everything already decided shrinks to a line of chrome.
  • Rule 2 — exactly one place is responsible for each choice, and the card that is asking is the card that is open.
  • Rule 5 — nothing is set by accident: the revision reason is a real field in the card, not a browser prompt, and Finalize stays disabled until it carries text.

Bends

  • Rule 8, the scale is fixed — the collapsed signed line runs at --t-micro throughout, which the grammar reserves as a floor rather than a body size. Argued: a signed result is a receipt, not something read mid-recitation.
  • Nothing else. It is the most conservative of the four, and the least useful once the queue is empty.

C — Reporting board

Keeps

  • Rule 6 — the pips say “3 of 4 sections in” without a sentence saying it.
  • Rule 9 — provisional totals are distinguished by weight and a dashed rule and the pip count, never by a lighter grey alone.

Bends

  • Rule 11 in spirit — the total is visible before the commit, but its four parts are not; they need a click. This is the candidate's real cost.
  • PRODUCT_FOUNDATION §10.4 — it draws placements before verification has passed. Mitigated by never printing a place number on a provisional row, but a ranked list still invites the reading. Ship this one only with the provisional treatment intact.

D — Ledger page

Keeps

  • Rule 1 — the record is the evidence and gets the paper; the controls sit at its foot as chrome.
  • Rule 5 — the gravity of the visual matches the gravity of the act, which is the cheapest accident-prevention there is.
  • It is the only candidate that gives the verification manifest a home, closing a gap the workbook already depends on.

Bends

  • Rule 8 — the total is set at --t-display in the serif, which no other in-app control uses. Argued: this is a document, and the app already gives the Mushaf its own type world.
  • Rule 1, read the other way — at roughly one card per 200px, chrome-to-evidence is the worst of the four once you are working through forty reciters rather than reading one.
