Tap the whole, drag the half

Six ways to build the same control, all sharing one gesture: press a number to award it, or hold and drag to land between two of them. Whole marks are the targets; halves are what the drag is for. Three are bars and three are boxes, so the question you actually asked — whole bar or number boxes — can be settled by using both.

All six are driven by one state machine. Press and the whole number under the pointer is taken. Keep holding past 220ms, or move more than 6px, and the control switches to half resolution — the readout turns green and the hint changes. Release commits. Keyboard: arrows step whole marks, shift+arrow steps halves.

The allocation switcher is real: START_OPTIONS filtered by MAX_IMPRESSION_MARKS gives Adu & Raagu exactly 5, 10, 15 or 20 marks, so those are the four cases any of these has to survive. Twenty is the one that breaks things.

What the switcher shows

Set the allocation to 20 and the popup to 440 and the difference stops being a matter of taste. Twenty-one cells in one row is the case every single-row variant has to answer for, and only two of the six answer it well.

One thing worth deciding before implementation. A contiguous bar (variant A) and a continuous track (variant E) both read as “you have this much of ten”, because everything below the value is filled. Separated boxes with a single selection (variant C) read as “you picked seven”. Those are different claims about what the control means, and only one of them is true: an Adu & Raagu mark is an award out of a maximum, not a choice from a list. That argues for cumulative fill in whichever shape wins.
