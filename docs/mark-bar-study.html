WCAG asks 3:1 of any graphical element that carries meaning. A fill that marks where the value sits carries meaning, so at 1.15:1 this is not a preference — the control is drawing something nobody can see.

02

Why the green feels electric

Converted to OKLCH, #2e9e83 is L 63, C 0.107, H 173°. That lightness is the problem, and it is the same problem as the contrast one: at L 63 white text lands at 3.32:1 and fails, while ink passes — and ink on a mid-saturated green is exactly the combination that reads as a stock analytics dashboard. Every ratio below is computed in your browser, not typed in.

Today, all twelveEvery green literal in global.css, sorted by lightness

Hue spans 19.5°. Five of them sit inside two points of lightness — one colour, re-picked by eye six times.

White text becomes legal at step 700 and never looks back. The shaded rows are the dead band where neither white nor ink reaches 4.5:1 — today's green sits just above it.

Four candidates

Each is a phone-width mock of the score row plus its bar. Drag the bar, or press it. All four use one green from one ramp, drop the halo, and put the numerals on the scale rather than in a gutter beneath it — those are defects, not variables. What differs is the shape of the gesture.

04

Square, or slightly wide?

This one is settled by scrolling, not by adjectives. Same six cards, same content — change the proportion and the corner and scroll the list. A square card makes the eye travel in two directions per item; a wide one fixes the horizontal so the scroll axis carries all the movement. corner-shape: superellipse() is applied where the browser supports it and quietly ignored where it does not.

The inner well follows the nesting rule Apple's shapes keep at every scale: inner radius = outer radius − padding. Set the outer to 20 and watch the well track it; a card whose inner corner repeats the outer radius is the detail that makes a rounded interface look approximate.

05

What actually changes

No scoring, storage or gesture semantics. One ramp replaces twelve literals, the fill becomes solid, and three declarations that make the glow are deleted. 

/* tokens: one ramp, one hue (OKLCH H 165) */
--g-100: #eef8f3;   --g-600: #54967a;
--g-200: #def0e7;   --g-700: #377b60;  /* fill; white text 5.04:1, and clears 3:1
                                          against the light AND the dark track */
--g-300: #c6e3d5;   --g-800: #26624b;  /* green text on a light surface, 7.16:1 */
--g-400: #a5cfbc;   --g-900: #1b4a38;
--g-500: #7cb39a;   --g-1000: #113326;

.mark-bar-fill
  background: var(--c-wash);            /* 1.15:1 */
  background: var(--g-700);             /* 4.46:1 */

.mark-bar-thumb
  box-shadow: 0 0 0 3px var(--c-wash);  /* the halo */

.mark-picker:hover, .mark-picker.is-open
  background: var(--c-wash);            /* the card lighting up */
  border-color: var(--c);
  border-color: var(--line-2);          /* weight, not colour */

.mark-tick-label
  top: 21px;                            /* a gutter under the track */
  inset: 0;                             /* on the scale, clipped in two layers */
