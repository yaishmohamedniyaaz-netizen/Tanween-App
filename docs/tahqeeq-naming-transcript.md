# Tahqeeq-App Naming Process: Complete Conversation Transcript

## Session Overview

**Project:** Tahqeeq-App — a Qur'an competition judging platform  
**Purpose:** Find an authentic Arabic brand name that reflects the app's unique value proposition (letter-level precision marking with full transparency)  
**Final Candidates:** 
- **Mizan Harf** (ميزان الحرف — "the scale of the letter")
- **Midad Sawt** (مداد الصوت — "the ink of the voice")

---

## Phase 1: Initial Exploration (Rounds 1-5)

### Request 1: Dictionary Access
**User:** "we need you to pull up an arabic dictionary classical arabic a compressed one with a billion words or somth. which would be best"

**Response:** Recommended Hans Wehr Dictionary (24,799 entries) as the best resource for classical Arabic with systematic organization. Created SQLite database queries to systematically explore candidates.

**Key Decision:** Moved beyond intuition-based naming to programmatic filtering with criteria:
- Letter safety (avoiding difficult consonants for Maldivian pronunciation)
- Syllable count (2-3 syllables for memorability)
- Skeleton collision detection (homophones using emphatic-folding)
- Semantic field relevance

### Candidates from Round 1
Single-word candidates focused on judge/arbitration vocabulary:
- Tahkeem (تحكيم)
- Taqweem (تقويم)
- Qisṭ (قسط)
- Muʿtamad (معتمد)

**User Feedback:** "sounds too similar. those vowel rules I dont agree with either."
- Rejected phonetic similarity to existing apps
- Rejected vowel-position rules as too restrictive
- Wants 100+ new candidates

### Round 2: Expanded Single-Word Pool (100+ words)
Generated candidates across semantic fields:
- Judge/arbitration
- Recording/documentation
- Marking/coloring
- Transparency/unveiling

**User Feedback:** "these risk sounding cringe... you ignored my judge word thing"
- Signal: Job-title coinages sound artificial
- Signal: Judge vocabulary is the core domain, not yet properly explored

### Round 3: Color & Marking Vocabulary
Explored words related to visual marking and color:
- Bayḍāʾ (white/clarity)
- Sawād (black/marking)
- Khaṭṭ (line/stroke)

**User Feedback:** "not associated well enough, or sound good enough"
- Poetic/metaphorical names weren't connecting to app function
- Single-word solutions weren't landing

---

## Phase 2: Pivot to Two-Word Constructions (Rounds 6-15)

### Critical Insight
User: "maybe soemthing like what ebglish deswithv paypal luke two word mix in arabic like zukashf or somethibg haha idk or judge word can we…"

**Key Signal:** Two-word combinations might work better than single words, but must be authentic Arabic (not English-Arabic hybrids).

### User Clarification
"maybe lets expore 2 wrod combos which are not cringe because musanif meaningn is not as level high as the tranpsecry or metaphor or symbolic ones for most striking description of what this app provides"

**Translation:** 
- Two-word iḍāfa (possessive) constructions are authentic (not cringe)
- Avoid literal job titles (Muṣannif = author)
- Need metaphorical/symbolic construction
- Should describe what the app *does* most strikingly

### The App's Unique Claim
User: "laḥn jalī, laḥn khafī, faṣāḥa categories... marks mistakes at character/letter level"

**Breakthrough:** The unique value isn't general fairness or judging — it's **documentary evidence at letter resolution**. Names should claim what makes this different from other judging systems.

### Early Two-Word Iḍāfa Candidates
Pattern: [Measured Thing] + [Measure/Tool/Metaphor]

- Mizan Harf (ميزان الحرف — scale of the letter)
- Midad Sawt (مداد الصوت — ink of the voice)
- Hafz Qawl (حفظ القول — preservation of speech)
- Tawtīq Laḥn (توثيق اللحن — documentation of errors)

## Phase 3: Semantic Refinement (Rounds 16-22)

### Exploring Different Semantic Fields

**1. Measurement & Documentary Evidence**
- Mizan (scale) — establishes precision metaphor
- Qadr (measure) — philosophical precision
- Tawzīn (weighing) — careful judgment

**2. Ink & Writing Metaphors**
- Midad (ink) — ephemeral sound → permanent record
- Qalem (pen) — marking/writing
- Kitāba (writing) — documentation

**3. Voice & Preservation**
- Sawt (voice) — the medium being marked
- Ṣawt (sound) — same root
- Talawwa (recitation) — the action

**4. Letter-Specific Symbolism**
- Harf (letter) — the unit of marking
- Harika (character) — same root, less poetic
- Nutq (enunciation) — the act

### Error Encountered: Oversaturation
By round 20-22, the user noted: "the Arabic lexicon under your constraints is close to exhausted for this meaning-space. Another 200 will be lower quality than these, not higher."

**Resolution:** User directed away from exhaustive search: "stop searching and start looking" — time to visualize names on the actual interface.

## Phase 5: Final Candidates & Comparison (Rounds 23-30)

### Mizan Harf (ميزان الحرف)

**Translation:** "The Scale of the Letter"

**Meaning Layers:**
- Mizan (scale/balance) — instrument for precise measurement
- Already in users' vocabulary (scales used to weigh recitations in traditional tajweed)
- Ḥarf (letter) — the unit being marked, the unique claim

**Advantages:**
1. Makes unique, measurable claim: "We weigh at the letter level"
2. No metaphor to explain — direct, technical language
3. Familiar word pair to Arabic speakers (classical scholarly usage)
4. Two-word iḍāfa is authentic, not manufactured
5. International positioning strong (scales/measuring = universal concept)
6. Tagline potential: "ميزان الحرف يسجل كل حرف" (The scale of the letter records every letter)

**Tone:** Authoritative, technical, claims superiority through precision

**User Statement:** "Mizan Harf — the scale of the letter — is the one I'd build on. It isn't a metaphor you have to explain, it's a **claim no competitor can make**."

---

### Midad Sawt (مداد الصوت)

**Translation:** "The Ink of the Voice"

**Meaning Layers:**
- Midad (ink) — traditionally, what makes writing permanent
- Sawt (voice) — the ephemeral thing being recorded
- Poetic compression: the app takes voice (which vanishes instantly) and writes it down (makes it permanent)

**Advantages:**
1. Tells the deepest story: ephemeral → permanent, voice → record
2. Highly poetic and emotionally resonant
3. Two-word iḍāfa is authentic
4. Metaphor is accessible ("ink of the voice" = "written record of speech")
5. Appeals to poets, scholars, and classical tradition
6. Tagline potential: "كل صوت مكتوب ودائم" (Every voice written and permanent)

**Tone:** Poetic, aspirational, emphasizes permanence and dignity of recording

**User Statement:** "Midād al-Ṣawt — the ink of the voice — is the more beautiful one, and the better story."

## Key Learnings & Errors Overcome

### Error 1: Over-Constraining Letter Choices
- **Initial approach:** Banned all gutturals (ḥ, ʿ, gh, q, ṣ, ḍ, ṭ, ẓ, th)
- **Problem:** Reduced candidate pool from 8,621 to 3,706
- **User rejection:** Implicit after rounds 6-8
- **Fix:** Relaxed to allow exactly one guttural per word
- **Result:** Reopened the semantic space

### Error 2: Phonetic Collisions
- **Problem:** Presented names too similar to existing apps (Muttasil ~ Tarteel, Mutqan ~ Motqan)
- **Fix:** Integrated web-search collision detection into filtering
- **Result:** Only presented truly unclaimed names in later rounds

### Error 3: Confusing "Authentic" with "Cringe"
- **Wrong approach:** Single-word coinages and naḥt blends felt artificial
- **User feedback:** "these risk sounding cringe"
- **Fix:** Moved to classical two-word iḍāfa constructions (the genuine scholarly tradition)
- **Result:** Names that feel native, not invented

### Error 4: Missing the Core Product Claim
- **Wrong approach:** Searching for names about recording, transparency, fairness generically
- **Breakthrough:** App's unique claim is letter-level precision marking
- **User insight:** "The unique value is documentary evidence at letter resolution"
- **Fix:** Reframed all candidates toward letter/voice vocabulary with measurement metaphors
- **Result:** Names that differentiate, not just brand

### Error 5: Pursuing Exhausted Semantic Fields
- **Problem:** Kept doing 150-200 word sweeps looking for one more perfect candidate
- **User feedback:** "the lexicon is close to exhausted... Another 200 will be lower quality"
- **User direction:** "stop searching and start looking"
- **Fix:** Stopped the expansion phase, moved to visualization phase
- **Result:** Acceptance that iteration had peaked, time for user judgment

## Naming Framework Used

### The Two-Word Iḍāfa Pattern

**Structure:** [Measured Thing] + [Measure/Tool/Metaphor]

**Why authentic:**
- Classical scholarly Arabic naming tradition (e.g., "Mukhtaar al-Ṣiḥāḥ" = Dictionary of Correct Words)
- Feels native, not manufactured
- Avoids English-Arabic hybrids
- Two-word names naturally extend to taglines

### Semantic Fields Tested

1. **Measurement & Precision:** Mizan (scale), Qadr (measure), Tawzīn (weighing)
2. **Documentation & Recording:** Tawtīq (documentation), Siǧill (record), Dabbara (recorded)
3. **Ink & Permanence:** Midad (ink), Qalem (pen), Kitāba (writing)
4. **Voice & Sound:** Sawt (voice), Tilawa (recitation), Nutq (enunciation)
5. **Letter & Character:** Harf (letter), Harika (character), Kashf (unveiling)
6. **Transparency & Unveiling:** Kashf (unveiling), Wāḍiḥ (clear), Ḍiyāʾ (light)
